## Why

CHG-258 classified every service's restart policy and pinned the classification in
`TestRestartBudget`: stateful trading services bound their retries (4 per 120 s) so a service that
cannot start "stops churning and waits for an operator, visibly, at `0/1`", while infrastructure
services retry forever because a stopped quorum, collector or alert consumer is a *silent* hole.
`ingestion` was classified as trading.

The measured case that prompted this review says the classification was wrong for that one service.
Ingestion lost its budget when the **bridge crashed** — a dependency that comes back — and again when
the DDL catalog it reads had not been applied yet: the catalog is applied by **a later step of the
same deploy** (S8, measured at 17 m 16 s in a certifying run). Both are transient within one deploy,
and bounded retries converted them into a manual `docker service update --force prod_ingestion` in
the runbook — the operator work the unbounded policy exists to remove. The anchor's rationale ("a
missing DDL catalog, a rejected credential") reads a late-arriving prerequisite as a permanent error.

The other seven bounded services keep their budget, deliberately: `fluss-*` and `flink-*` fail on
state and catalog errors a restart loop does not fix, and `execution-bridge` emits orders, where
churn is the safety case, not the cost.

## What changed

- `code/01_platform/01_docker/docker-stack.yml` — `ingestion`'s `deploy` block overrides the
  inherited `restart_policy` sub-map with `condition: any` and `delay: 30s`, no `max_attempts`. The
  comment records why (a transient prerequisite, applied later in the same deploy) and why 30 s
  rather than the anchor's 5 s: the failure it must survive lasts as long as the DDL step, and the
  JVM should not boot every five seconds while it waits. The sub-map override replaces the anchor's
  wholesale — the merge-key note at the top of the file explains that shallow-merge behaviour.
- `code/01_platform/04_scripts/tests/test_09_stack.py` — `ingestion` moves from `BOUNDED` to
  `UNBOUNDED` with a comment carrying the reasoning; the classification test still fails if a
  service is in neither set or in both.
- `docs/05_deployment/PROD_VM_PROVISIONING.md` — S8 no longer instructs the operator to
  `docker service update --force prod_ingestion` (ingestion now heals itself after the catalog
  exists), and the vendor-credential trap states the new shape of a permanent failure: `0/1`,
  restarting every 30 s, rather than a stopped task.

## Measured

- `docker stack config -c code/01_platform/01_docker/docker-stack.yml` → **rc=0**, 20 services, and
  the resolved policies: `ingestion {condition: any, delay: 30s}` (no `max_attempts`),
  `flink-jobmanager` and `execution-bridge` `{condition: any, delay: 5s, max_attempts: 4,
  window: 2m0s}`, `eod-scheduler` / `nautilus` / `otel-collector-logs` `{condition: any}`. Resolved
  by Docker's own parser, not by reading the YAML.
- `python3 -m pytest test_09_stack.py -q` → **63 passed** (count unchanged by the move).
- `python3 -m pytest test_08_local_compose_prod.py -q` → **21 passed** (PROD-007, unchanged file).
- Mutation: `ingestion` put back into `BOUNDED` while the stack no longer bounds it →
  `test_trading_services_stop_retrying_after_a_bounded_budget` fails with `AssertionError:
  ingestion: a trading service must bound its retries`, 1 failed / 62 passed. The mutation was
  applied against asserted-unique anchors and the file was restored byte-identically (`cmp`).

## Not verified

The behaviour the change is *for* — an ingestion task that failed on a late prerequisite succeeding
on a later retry without operator action — cannot be shown on the laptop: ingestion has no
`ARROW_*` vendor credentials here, so it never reaches the schema failure this policy addresses and
sits at `0/1` for the credential reason instead. VM-day evidence: deploy, let ingestion fail before
the DDL step, apply the catalog, and confirm the service reaches `1/1` with no `--force`. Also
unmeasured: how Swarm spaces retries under `delay: 30s` beyond the first attempt.
