## Why

Operator decision, 2026-09-21: the platform's VMs are 250 GB, so the provisioning floor has to be
250 GB. A floor larger than the machine the platform actually runs on fails the deploy gate on
arrival, and a gate that is wrong about the target hardware is worse than no gate.

## What this changes, and what it deliberately does not

- `prod_node_check.py`'s `DEFAULT_DISK_MIN_GB`, its usage example, and the template's top-level and
  per-row `disk_min_gb` — all four now read 250.
- Ten documents that state the allocation as current policy. Historical records — the 2026-08-25 and
  2026-09-06 plans, and CHG-082/246/264/278 — are left exactly as written, because they record what
  was true when they were written.
- **`DEC-025` is annotated, not rewritten**: the decision cell carries a dated `(REVISED 2026-09-21,
  CHG-280 …)` note, the way `DEC-010` records its own supersession. A decision record says what was
  decided and when; silently editing one destroys the only evidence that it changed.
- **`RISK-014` stays `High` and stays `Open`, and its text now says the reduction raises the chance
  it materialises.** The risk is that seven-day capacity exceeds the per-VM allocation; making the
  allocation smaller moves the risk in the wrong direction. The number in the risk changed; its
  severity was not touched.
- The mitigation that actually bounds the data is unchanged and remains the safety net: measured
  capacity model, then warning/critical/stop thresholds, with no expiry while EOD offload is
  unverified (`DEC-026`).

## What this does not claim

250 GB is a floor, not a sizing result. It is a **policy** number: the measured rehearsal footprint
is 15.46 GB of RAM, and the disk figure stays `EVIDENCE-BLOCKED` until `PERF-PROD-60000-001` runs on
the real stack. This record makes the platform's floor agree with the machines it will run on; it
does not assert that 250 GB is sufficient for seven days of production retention.
